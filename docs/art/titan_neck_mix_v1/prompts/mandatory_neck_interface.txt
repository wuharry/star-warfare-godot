MANDATORY ADDENDUM — MIXED-ARMOR NECK INTERFACE

Append this addendum to the complete, pinned armor-runtime integration prompt.
Do not replace, silently rewrite, or change the SHA of that base prompt. This
addendum is both an art requirement and an engineering acceptance contract;
prompt compliance alone is not evidence that the runtime asset is safe.

Preserve the approved helmet design, proportions, rounded visor, crown, frame,
ear areas and the series' hand-painted shading. Any requested helmet redesign
must stop at the original neck interface. A helmet may be equipped with a body
from another armor set. Its neck cannot depend on its matching body's collar
to hide a seam, an unfinished patch, a missing face or an incorrectly colored
undersuit.

Discover the neck from ALL surfaces of the TRUE ORIGINAL source mesh, using position-welded
triangle connected components and their active named head/torso bone influences.
Do not identify it from the proposed target, an editable target whitelist or a
hand-authored list of convenient vertex IDs. Include every seam-duplicate UV
vertex that shares a protected physical position. For Titan the source-derived
component has 12 physical positions, 24 UV vertices and 12 triangles, with
Bip01 Head and Bip01 Spine1 influences. Those are cross-checks of the discovered
source, not permission to hard-code the selection for other helmets.

Keep all protected source neck positions, body-interface anchors, UVs, triangle
indices and winding, bone bindings and normalized skin weights unchanged.
Preserve all source neck faces exactly once. Do not delete, reverse, duplicate,
bridge, shrink or displace them. Apply this protection after every face-shaping,
alignment, export and compile stage. An overall 15–20% silhouette/UV budget
does not permit any change to the protected neck interface. Added helmet arc
subdivision vertices must not move or replace the original neck component.

For Titan's native diffuse atlas, render the actual neck UV chart as opaque, near-black
matte undersuit cloth, continuous through all triangle edges and seam padding.
Use subtle broad cloth shading. Do not paint gray metal, bright wear, blue/gold
armor, exposed skin or transparent pixels inside this chart. Preserve the layout
and design of every other chart. For the current Titan layout, the protected neck
lies around U=0.013–0.562 and V=0.878–0.982; derive the exact triangle coverage
from the original UVs rather than treating this bounding rectangle as a mask.
Keep the native image's full dimensions and archive every attempt and complete
sent prompt. Adopt its PNG bytes unchanged. No scripted painting, recoloring,
cropping, resizing or compositing of artwork is permitted.

Correct the source texture and geometry. Do not hide a failure with material
tint, transparency, extra ambient darkness, a camera crop, an enlarged matching
collar or a lowered whole helmet. Use the normal runtime material and lighting.
Check actual equipped material samples, including tint, UV transforms and alpha.
Geometry, UV, named skin/weights, exact neck faces and opacity are hard shared
compiler requirements. Charcoal color limits are design-specific: the shared
pipeline enables them only with neck_charcoal_required=true; other designs keep
their approved source cloth palette. Titan's dedicated compiler always enforces
its strict charcoal limits. Do not disable or relax Titan's pinned material or
interface limits to make a candidate pass.

The build/compile gate must independently derive and validate the original neck
component. A failed position, UV, topology, named skin or weight check must stop
the build before promotion. Runtime validation must inspect the compiled SCN
and exported GLB, not just target JSON or a same-suit preview.

Reject non-finite values explicitly BEFORE ArrayMesh/GPU upload and before
distance/budget comparisons: NaN and infinity in source/candidate positions,
UVs, weights, transforms, Skin binds, material tint/UV parameters or sampled
pixels must fail closed. NaN can make a comparison evaluate false; a small-error
assertion alone does not validate the input. Validate required parts and triangle
counts before packing, saving or exporting any runtime file.

Prove the guard rejects independent, in-memory negative fixtures, including a
moved anchor, changed UV, changed bone binding/weight, removed/reversed/duplicated
neck face, added bridge and transparent chart. For Titan/charcoal-enabled checks,
also reject gray cloth and include the frozen defective
native atlas on known-valid original geometry so an unrelated geometry failure
cannot make a material test appear to work. Fixtures must actually change the
tested channel. Preserve real source assets and the user's save data. In the
shared pipeline, separately prove that valid opaque non-charcoal palettes remain
allowed when charcoal is not enabled, while transparent pixels and corrupt
interfaces are still rejected. Exercise the real production writer: failure must
preserve an existing destination's bytes and must not create a previously missing
output scene.

Add real non-finite regression fixtures, not only labels or mocked return values.
Confirm the invalid value survives in the actual packed channel or decoded
ArrayMesh, then prove rejection at the production validation stage. For channels
tested before upload, exercise that same production pre-upload validator. Include
true-original source corruption as well as candidate corruption. The real writer
must also refuse a candidate with a retained NaN vertex without overwriting an
existing scene or creating a missing destination. Preserve each earlier test
report and append the final finite-safety report as separate evidence.

For Titan, equip the delivered helmet with ALL 29 body IDs (0–28). Test the real
gameplay and store equipment paths. Sample idle, running and reloading at three
times each: 29 bodies × 9 poses = 261 actual posed checks. Compare the protected
posed neck with the true-original source, not the new target. Capture at least
35 normal-resource neck close-ups: eight representative bodies × front/quarter/
side/rear neutral views, plus Viper-body idle/run/reload quarter views. Include
the low collars, a same-set control and late Call of Mini bodies. Inspect these
renders for background holes, collar separation, clipping and visible unfinished
cloth. If coverage still fails after interface restoration, repair the actual
skinned geometry and repeat the complete checks; painting alone is insufficient.

Bind all reports and captures to actual resource SHA256 values, renderer,
dimensions and unchanged/restored real save state. Preserve before evidence and
failed attempts. Do not reuse stale prior-revision PASS reports as current proof.
Shared-compiler and Titan-dedicated-compiler integration does not prove that
every other custom compiler has been migrated or every existing asset has been
revalidated. Name the actual paths and tests completed. Engineering PASS, human
visual inspection, user art acceptance and device
performance are separate statuses. Leave user art acceptance pending until the
user evaluates the delivered result.

Record the limits of human inspection: reviewed frame count, resolution,
background, selected camera angles and any weapon/armor occlusion. Inspect
collar overlaps as well as obvious background holes. A charcoal luminance
threshold is an upper limit, not proof of exact original-cloth color matching.
Document any remaining rectangular source-neck outline, visible collar overlap
or brighter cloth instead of presenting a contract PASS as a perfect appearance.
