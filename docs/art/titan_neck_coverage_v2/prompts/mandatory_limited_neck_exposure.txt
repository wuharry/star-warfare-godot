MANDATORY ADDENDUM — COMPACT HELMET-TO-COLLAR FIT

Append this addendum to the existing fixed armor integration prompt and the
original neck-interface addendum. Do not rewrite either historical base prompt.

The direct user requirement is a short, mostly concealed neck seal matching the
existing armor family. A large exposed black neck tube, even if opaque and
correctly skinned, is a visual failure. Do not present the old original-neck
position contract alone as proof that the helmet-to-body fit is acceptable.

Before authoring, inspect the actual original helmets and the currently accepted
Viper/Fortune/Tank family in the same engine camera, body, pose and lighting. Use
the real current Titan mesh as the before comparison. Preserve the approved round
gold pressure visor, helmet scale and continuous lower frame. Aim for a small
sealed collar seam rather than a tall visible neck column. All body IDs must be
compatible; a tall collar on one matching suit cannot stand in for mixed bodies.

Keep the original source neck component fixed, including physical seam
duplicates, UVs, faces, active named bones and weights. Fit the actual shell over
this protected interface. Never hide a mismatch by darkening the whole material,
using transparency, enlarging every body collar or changing the head pivot.

For Titan v7 only, the authorized change is an overall downward rest-space
fit plus a small explicitly recorded lower-frame extension. The 24 source-neck
UV vertices remain exact. The runtime shell_fit records the overall delta_y and
every lower_frame_offsets original physical seam group; no incomplete duplicate
group, overlapping group, neck point or unknown vertex is allowed. Extra lower
offsets must be negative, at most 110 mm, and below Y=1.58 after the overall fit.
Keep the reviewed gold visor vertices free from these local extra offsets.
The 40 existing arc midpoints inherit the mean extra offset of their two
original parents; no independent new midpoint shape is authorized.
Keep all 334 UV coordinates, 164 triangles,
3 head charts, 28 bones, indices and weights exact to delivered v6. Verify the
complete result against the true original 20% geometry/dimension budget. The
five selected PNGs are byte-for-byte inherited; this revision is not a new
imagegen attempt. Keep its inherited-native provenance typed and unambiguous.

Acceptance requires both interface invariants and actual exposed-neck coverage.
Measure rendered visible neck pixels using an isolated mesh/component mask with
the four equipped armor pieces still occluding it. After EVERY animation/reload
pose, hide weapons, backpacks and reload debris; only the selected helmet,
body, hands and feet may occlude this diagnostic. Do not let a weapon mask a
helmet-to-collar gap. Compare matched views and
poses against the frozen before Titan and the actual family references. Record
the measurement definition, denominator, camera, renderer, thresholds, resource
SHA256 and every failed row. Thresholds must be chosen from family evidence
before adoption; never relax them merely because an authored candidate fails.
Both equipped-geometry and complete-head mask passes must prove completion and
exact source/rendered surface and triangle-index counts for every sample.
Actual nonindexed triangle surfaces require an explicit full vertex sequence;
never silently omit them. Reject any paired engine log with a script or parse
error, even when a partial JSON report claims PASS.

Check all 29 body IDs with idle, running and reload samples. Review front,
quarter, side and rear closeups, including low collars and weapon-occluded poses.
Quantified sampled coverage is not a guarantee for every camera or animation
time. Inspect remaining black seams, background holes, jaw/collar clipping,
continuous visor/frame silhouette and skin deformation in actual images.

Future armor pipelines must retain this visible-neck coverage check in addition
to the original-neck save gate. A valid source interface plus a visibly exposed
long neck must be rejected. Keep engineering PASS, visual inspection, user art
acceptance, browser interaction and mobile performance as separate statuses.
