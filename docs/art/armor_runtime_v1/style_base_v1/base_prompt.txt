USE CASE: A new armor design belonging to this project's ORIGINAL low-poly mobile-game armor family. This is a game-proportioned construction reference, not a tall cinematic character illustration.

REFERENCE HIERARCHY
The raw original game captures are the authority for proportions, silhouette, plate scale, texture style and visual detail density. Match the target armor's original FRONT and SIDE views as well as its three-quarter view. A separate newer concept may supply specified design motifs and color choices only; never inherit its human body proportions, realistic rendering or surface complexity. Never use a rejected generated model as a reference.

LOCKED PROPORTIONS
An adult sci-fi soldier in the exaggerated proportions of the supplied original game meshes: very large armored helmet, short neck almost hidden by the collar, compact torso, short sturdy legs, thick forearms, simple gloved hands and substantial boots. Copy the TARGET original's exact relationship between helmet main shell, shoulder span, chest, waist, pelvis, knees and boots. The original sample family is approximately 2.5 to 3.1 MAIN-HELMET lengths tall, not an anatomical head-count prescription; each target's own views take precedence. Do not stretch the torso, slenderize limbs or shrink the helmet to fit a realistic human. Do not turn the helmet into a broad spherical toy head. Its front height/width, side depth, brow projection and chin taper must each follow the original target separately. Decorative fins, ears and collar do not determine the size of the main helmet shell.

MODELING LANGUAGE VISIBLE IN THE IMAGE
Use a small number of broad, deliberately shaped faceted surfaces. Large crown planes, a clearly angled brow, tapered cheeks, a compact integrated jaw, broad shoulder caps, simple breastplate masses, short gauntlets, single large shin plates and wedge-like boots. Each armor has its own silhouette; do not put one generic helmet on every suit. Keep the original target's important spikes/fins and characteristic plate breaks only where specified. Breaks between plates must be physically coherent and shallow. Glass, cheek returns and brow belong to one fitted helmet face; no floating face mask, detached brow shelf, open gap behind a glass card or separate giant square cheek blocks. Show a believable side profile with one clear continuous shell. Smaller panel lines and bevel highlights are painted surface details, not stacks of new geometry.

TEXTURE AND COLOR LANGUAGE
Match the supplied raw ORIGINAL game assets: hand-painted diffuse colors with broad soft light-to-dark gradients on each large plate, selective thin pale edge highlights and restrained dark seams. The paint supplies readable volume like an unlit game texture; avoid directional studio PBR shading. Armor has simple muted painted metal, dark flexible joint areas and a single controlled visor/emissive accent. Visor reflections are one or two large clean painted reflection shapes. Use very limited inset ornaments/vents. Retain generous quiet areas on the shell. No dense scratches, photoreal brushed metal, micro-grain, realistic grime, hundreds of fasteners, heavy weathering, high-frequency hex patterns over the whole character, bloom or polished collectible-statue finish. Also avoid blank flat vertex colors, cel-shaded comic outlines or voxel blocks: the original has painted depth and a few carefully placed crisp seams.

PRESENTATION
Present it as a small low-poly 3D game asset in neutral rest pose, arms down and slightly clear of the torso, entire feet visible. Simple uniform dark background, orthographic or near-orthographic views, consistent character size and ground line. Readable large forms at gameplay size; clean larger output resolution does not mean more surface detail. Follow the requested view layout. No weapon, props, logos, text, UI or labels unless specifically requested. Do not beautify into a taller or more realistic character.

PER-ARMOR ADD-ON REQUIRED
Supply target original views, main-shell width/height/depth and attachment extents as separate measurements; identity silhouette; helmet/glass type; main paint, joints and accent colors; approved new design motifs; retained body features; and requested views. Change these identity fields, not the locked visual language. For visorless helmets, do not invent glass. Preserve original family proportions even when referencing Halo/Destiny-style armor motifs.

WHAT THIS OUTPUT IS
This bitmap is a proposed construction/style reference. It is not a UV atlas, a skinned 3D mesh or proof of exact geometric matching. It must be compared with original front/side game captures before building a new production model.
